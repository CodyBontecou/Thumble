Read the skill, workflow, quality bar, art-direction acceptance criteria, critique 1, and designer notes 2. ## Pass 1 — composition

Both orientations remain deliberately composed. Landscape establishes two stations with an asymmetric movement/camera arrangement, narrow quiet bridge, and an immediately identifiable four-image ability group. Portrait reorders those functions into upper modifier rails and lower paired stations. Its middle remains connective rather than an ornamental focal point. Shoulder, utility, and stick-click groups are readable; no shell clipping or new control overlap is visible.

The prior major finding is resolved. The landscape ability well now has a clear band of recessed material above its circular edge and crimson top cut. In the 2× dark hero, the horizontal inset contour visibly clears both; it no longer reads as an accidental doubled seam. The documented 5.5 px clearance to the crimson cut and 9.5 px to the circle agree with the rendered separation. The same correction is visible in all eight landscape panels. Native positions and group alignment appear unchanged.

The narrowed light-portrait spine also resolves the prior balance refinement. It reads as a connector between the upper and lower rails rather than a heavy independent center bar. Both portrait appearances keep the ability group and analog stations ahead of passive decoration in the visual hierarchy.

## Pass 2 — material and craft

Steel plate faces, dark recessed stations, cool contour lines, restrained brush marks, and sparse crimson cuts form a coherent material system. The brighter light plate and darker engraved outlines are authored separately from dark mode. Native control highlights and compact shadows agree with the upper-left raised-surface lighting. The repaired contour does not introduce a kink or broken highlight. Full-color icons remain square, visibly uncropped, and clear of text overlays. No new raster artifacts, accidental seams, or misplaced decorative marks are visible.

The original framing is distinct from recognizable console hardware and from Blizzard's UI frame language. It is a restrained functional instrument rather than a literal armor or weapon replica. No badge, Warcraft wordmark, faction crest, character image, or borrowed game-UI border is visible.

## Pass 3 — interaction

All four states are meaningful in both appearances and orientations. Pressed utility faces darken/recess, while active faces retain stronger outer crimson boundaries and raised steel faces. Analog controls distinguish a depressed center from sustained ring feedback. Disabled neutralizes crimson emphasis and dims the icons without erasing their group structure. The native size response in pressed snapshots does not introduce authored layout drift. Held modifiers retain physical legends and consistent feedback.

### Finding 1 — minor, optional: image-face pressed/active distinction is narrower than utility distinction

- **Affected panels:** pressed versus active ability faces in all four orientation/appearance combinations, especially the portrait thumbnail pairs.
- **Visible evidence:** icon artwork occupies most of each face, so state is communicated primarily through crimson perimeter weight and shadow rather than the broader face-value changes visible on utility buttons. The states remain distinguishable side by side; this is not a major finding.
- **Likely cause:** preserving authentic full-color imagery and the existing native icon frame limits the exposed material surface.
- **Concrete correction:** if refined later, strengthen the pressed inner edge versus active outer edge within the current frame while preserving the entire image and clear margin. The measurable outcome should be immediate recognition of pressed versus active in caption-free, side-by-side 1× crops. No correction is required for this visual pass.
